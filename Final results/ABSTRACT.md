Resting-state EEG is a promising biomarker for early Parkinson's disease (PD), yet the cross-cohort generalization of graph-based models remains challenging. Across the three publicly available OpenNeuro PD datasets (230 participants, 141 PD and 89 controls), graph encoders exhibit poor transfer between sites. Site-specific factors, including electrode montage, acquisition hardware, and demographic variability, appear to dominate disease-related neural signatures. Thus, there is an urgent need for models that generalize reliably across heterogeneous PD-EEG cohorts. To address this, we propose GNN-ProtoNet.

Each EEG recording is represented as a 64-channel functional graph constructed from top-k phase-locking connectivity, with multimodal spectral, temporal, and complexity-based node descriptors. Two graph neural encoders, GCN and GAT, are trained under an episodic prototypical-network objective and fused by late combination. During inference, K labeled samples from an unseen subject's recording are used to update the class prototypes prior to scoring the remaining samples.

We evaluated the approach via leave-one-dataset-out cross-validation with K=5 samples per class. The GCN attains 94.47% accuracy and 0.9845 AUC; the GCN+GAT ensemble reaches 98.22% accuracy and 0.9961 AUC, correctly identifying 226 of 230 subjects. Ablation reveals that prototype calibration drives most of the gain, since neither encoder generalizes to test data without it. A single support example per class already yields 86.7% accuracy, and performance saturates at K=5.

To our knowledge, this work introduces the first graph-prototypical framework for PD-EEG classification evaluated across the full set of publicly available PD EEG datasets. The five-sample calibration overhead is low enough to remain clinically practical.
